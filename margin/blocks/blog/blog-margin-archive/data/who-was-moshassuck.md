On one corner near my house, two signs share a pole. One names a family that owned the land in the nineteenth century. The other names a river, in a word from the Narragansett language that was here long before either the family or the street.

<aside>What the word means is still argued about. I have been given four meanings so far, by four people who were each quite sure.</aside>

Street names are the slowest-changing thing in a city. Buildings come down and roads are widened, but a name, once it is on the signs and the deeds and the maps, can last for centuries. They are a record of who had the right to name things, and when.

This month I am collecting the oldest names I can find on the signs. If your corner has one, send me a picture.
