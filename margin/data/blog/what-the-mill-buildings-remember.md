Every mill along our rivers faces the water, even the ones that have not used it for a hundred years. That is the first thing to know about them: they were built around a wheel, and the wheel needed a fall of water, so the mill stood where the river dropped.

<aside>The bell towers are a clue too. The bell told the workers when to start and when to stop, long before anyone owned a watch.</aside>

The second thing is that they are long and narrow because light was the only light. Before electricity, a weaver needed a window within a few yards of her loom, so the buildings are rows of tall windows with the floors stacked behind them.

> The buildings were shaped by the work; the work is gone, and the shape stays.

Now they are apartments and studios and a brewery, and the windows that were made for weavers let the light into kitchens. Walk along the river on a spring evening and every one of them is lit.
