Cochineal opens straight onto the courtyard, under the bougainvillea that Theo's mother planted the week we bought the house. Its walls are washed in the dusty red the Vásquez family once sold by the sack, mixed by a painter from the barrio who still remembers the old recipe.

The ceiling is the house's own: beams of sabino wood that we took down, cleaned and put back one by one. In May, when Oaxaca is at its hottest, the thick adobe and the shade of the arcade keep it the coolest room in the house.
