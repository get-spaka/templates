Eugène Jansson grew up poor on Södermalm, the high island at the south of Stockholm, and painted its views for more than a decade. Critics called him "the blue painter." His nights are never black: the bay of Riddarfjärden glows, the gaslights of Hornsgatan curve away like a necklace, and the sky keeps a little of the day. The three paintings in this room were made between 1896 and 1902, from the same few streets above the water.
