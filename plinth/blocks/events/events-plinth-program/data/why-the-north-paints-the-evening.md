Between 1885 and 1915, painters in Denmark, Sweden and Norway turned away from the bright noon of French Impressionism and toward the hour after sunset. Signe Aaltonen, who curated Long Light, traces how it happened: the summer colony at Skagen, the Swedish painters back from Paris, a mountain seen on a skiing trip.

She starts from Krøyer's Summer Evening on Skagen's Southern Beach, and ends in the last room with the white nights. The talk lasts an hour, with time for questions, and is followed by a glass of wine on the pier, weather permitting.
