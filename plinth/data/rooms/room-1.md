In the 1880s a colony of painters gathered at Skagen, a fishing village where two seas meet at the northern tip of Jutland. By day they painted the fishermen. In the evenings they painted each other, walking the long southern beach while sea and sky turned the same pale blue. P. S. Krøyer called it "the blue hour," and the name stuck. This room hangs his two largest beach scenes beside Anna Ancher's small blue room, where the same light comes in through a window and lands on a girl at her crochet.
