The white church of Røros, the copper-mining town where Sohlberg lived from 1902, stands over its graveyard at night. The crosses and the graves are dark; the church takes the last light and glows a cold green-white, and behind it the hills and the sky are the deep blue of a winter evening.

Sohlberg painted the church many times, by day and by night. This version, with its tilted crosses and its single lit wall, is the most still. It hangs here as the way into the mountains: the same blue sky you will see in the next painting, over a town instead of a range.
