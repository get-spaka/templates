A man and a woman on a veranda above a lake, at midsummer, near midnight. They stand apart, each at a pillar, looking at each other or not quite. The lake behind them is pale and perfectly still; the light is the light of the white nights, which makes everything visible and nothing sharp.

Richard Bergh was a painter, a critic and later the director of the Nationalmuseum, and he wanted to paint a Nordic mood rather than a story. Two friends sat for it, and he worked on the picture across two summers. It is the largest painting in the show, and the one that gives the last room its name.
