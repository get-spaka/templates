The same water two years later, and much larger. The view is nearly the one in Moonlight Evening, but now the city is a thin dark band in the middle of the canvas, and above and below it everything is blue: a sky of long ribbons of cloud, a bay of reflected lamps.

Jansson exhibited it in 1898, and it was given to the Nationalmuseum the next year by a group of the city's art lovers. It is the painting that made his name. Stand to the side and the brushstrokes in the water look like ridges of blue thread; stand in front and the lights of the quays seem to tremble.
