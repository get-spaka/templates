Two women walk away from us along the edge of the sea: Anna Ancher, the painter, and Marie Krøyer, the painter's wife, arm in arm after supper. It is past nine on a June evening, and Krøyer has let the sand, the water and the sky run into one pale blue, broken only by a line of surf and the white of their dresses.

He made studies on the beach for weeks and finished the canvas in his studio the following winter, which is why it feels both quick and composed. Look at the footprints in the foreground, and at the yellow sash, the only warm color left in the picture. Krøyer called this time of day the blue hour. The exhibition begins with it because nearly every painter in these rooms was chasing the same thing: the moment when the light has gone from the land but not yet from the sky.
