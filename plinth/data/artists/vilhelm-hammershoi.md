Vilhelm Hammershøi was born in Copenhagen in 1864 and lived there nearly all his life. He entered the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts at fifteen, and studied too with Peder Severin Krøyer, whose summer evenings open this exhibition.

From 1898 he and his wife Ida lived at Strandgade 30, an old merchant's house near the harbor, and its rooms became his great subject. He painted them over and over, nearly bare, with a few pieces of furniture, a closed door, a window, and often Ida with her back turned. His palette was narrow, grays and whites and browns, and his touch so even that his pictures seem to hold their breath.

He was admired abroad in his lifetime, by Rilke among others, then nearly forgotten for half a century. He died of throat cancer in 1916, at fifty-one. Today his quiet rooms draw crowds wherever they hang.
