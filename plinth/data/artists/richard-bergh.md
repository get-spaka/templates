Richard Bergh was born in Stockholm in 1858, the son of the landscape painter Edvard Bergh. He studied at the Royal Swedish Academy of Fine Arts and in Paris, where he became one of the young Swedish painters who rebelled against the Academy's teaching and founded the Artists' Association in 1886.

He painted portraits of his friends among the writers and painters of his generation, and a few large works of what he called the Nordic mood: figures in landscapes, made still by the long light of the northern summer. Nordic Summer Evening, which he worked on across two summers, is the best known.

Bergh was also a writer and a teacher, and an advocate of the new Swedish painting. In 1915 he became the director of the Nationalmuseum in Stockholm and rehung its collections. He died there in 1919.
