Anna Brøndum was born in 1859 in Skagen, where her parents kept the inn at which the visiting painters stayed. She grew up among them, and against the custom of the time for a woman, decided to become one. She studied drawing in Copenhagen with Vilhelm Kyhn, and later in Paris with Pierre Puvis de Chavannes.

In 1880 she married the painter Michael Ancher, and they made their home in Skagen for the rest of their lives. While the men of the colony painted the sea and the fishermen, she painted interiors: kitchens, sewing rooms, the blue room of the inn, and the women working in them. Her real subject was light, how it falls through a window and lies on a wall or a floor, and she painted it with a freedom close to the French painters she admired.

She kept painting into old age and died in Skagen in 1935. Her house is now a museum, with its rooms kept as she left them.
