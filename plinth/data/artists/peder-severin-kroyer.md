Peder Severin Krøyer was born in Stavanger in 1851 and raised in Copenhagen by his mother's sister and her husband, a zoologist, which may explain the patience of his eye. He trained at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts, then spent four years in Paris, where he studied with Léon Bonnat and saw the Impressionists paint outdoors.

In 1882 he came to Skagen for the first time, and for the rest of his life he returned every summer. He painted the fishermen hauling their boats, the artists' long dinners at the inn, and above all the beach in the evening, when sea and sky turn the same blue. He married the painter Marie Triepcke in 1889, and she became his most painted model.

Krøyer was celebrated in his lifetime, the most fashionable portraitist in Copenhagen, but his last years were marked by illness and the end of his marriage. He died at Skagen in 1909. His beach scenes are now among the best-loved paintings in Denmark, and two of the largest open this exhibition.
