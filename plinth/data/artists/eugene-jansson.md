Eugène Jansson was born in Stockholm in 1862 and grew up on Södermalm, the steep island at the south of the city, in a family of small means. Illness interrupted his schooling, and he studied painting at the Academy in Stockholm only briefly, learning more from the work of Edvard Munch and the French painters he saw in exhibitions.

From the early 1890s he painted the views from the hills where he lived: the bay of Riddarfjärden, the streets of Södermalm, the city across the water, nearly always at dusk or at night. In these paintings the sky, the water and the lamps are all one deep, moving blue, and critics soon called him "the blue painter."

After 1904 he changed subjects entirely, painting athletes and bathers in bright daylight. He died in Stockholm in 1915. His blue nights now hang in the Nationalmuseum, in Prince Eugen's house at Waldemarsudde and in the Thiel Gallery, his early patron's collection.
