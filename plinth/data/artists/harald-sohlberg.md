Harald Sohlberg was born in Kristiania, now Oslo, in 1869. He trained first as a decorative painter, then studied with several Norwegian painters, among them Erik Werenskiold, and spent time in Copenhagen, Weimar and Paris.

His landscapes are built with great care, often over years of drawings and studies, and painted in clear, unmixed colors: a white church at night, a fisherman's cottage, a table on a veranda at midsummer. In 1899 he saw the Rondane mountains on a skiing trip, and the sight stayed with him; from 1902 he lived for several years in the mining town of Røros, which he painted again and again under snow.

Winter Night in the Mountains, finished in 1914, became the most famous painting in Norway. Sohlberg died in Oslo in 1935.
