A will speaks only for what you own in your name alone when you die. Much of what a family owns never passes through it.

## What passes outside a will

- **Accounts with a named beneficiary**: retirement accounts, life insurance and payable-on-death bank accounts go to whoever is named on the form, whatever the will says.
- **Property owned jointly with survivorship**: most married couples' houses in Virginia pass to the survivor by the deed itself.
- **Anything in a trust**, which the trust's own terms govern.

## What to do instead

Make a list of every account and who is named on it, and check it against your will. The most common problem we see is a beneficiary form that names a former spouse, or a child who has since died.

If you'd like us to check, bring the list to a first meeting: it's usually the most useful forty-five minutes of the whole plan.
