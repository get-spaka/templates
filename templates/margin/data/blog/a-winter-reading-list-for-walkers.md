In January the walks get shorter and the reading gets longer. These are the books I give people who ask how to start looking at a city, in the order I would read them.

<aside>All of them are at the public library, and most of them have a waiting list in January. Ask early.</aside>

1. **A field guide to the street,** for the names of everything you walk past and never knew the word for: curb cuts, cornices, manholes, the little iron rings for tying up a horse.
2. **A history of one city block,** for how much can happen on a few hundred yards of ground in two hundred years.
3. **A book of walks by a writer who never drove,** for the pleasure of going slowly, and noticing.
4. **An atlas of old maps,** for laying the past over the present and seeing what has moved.

> Reading about a city is walking it in a chair, and on the coldest days that is enough.

The other three are in next week's letter, along with the books you sent me.
