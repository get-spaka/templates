A year ago I set myself a rule: every street in the city, on foot, at least once, before the next August. I kept a paper map on the kitchen wall and colored each street in as I walked it.

On Thursday I colored in the last one, a dead end of six houses behind the rail yard, and stood at the end of it for a while, not sure what to do next.

<aside>The map is still on the wall. The colored pencil I started with is a stub the length of a thumbnail.</aside>

## What a whole city teaches you

Walking every street is a different thing from walking the good ones. You see the parts no one shows a visitor: the streets that end in a fence, the ones that change their name halfway, the corners where every house was built in the same year by the same man.

> You don't know a city until you have walked the streets you had no reason to walk.

I would do it again. I would also, next time, buy better shoes in January rather than in May.
