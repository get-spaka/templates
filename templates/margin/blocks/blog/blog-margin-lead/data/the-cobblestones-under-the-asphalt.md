Under most of the old streets near the river, the stones are still there. They were laid by hand, each one set in sand and knocked into place, and when the cars came the city simply covered them over and kept going.

Where the asphalt is thin, they come back. First a crack, then a rounded gray back showing through, then, after a hard winter, a whole patch of them, as if the street were shedding a skin.

<aside>Paving stones like these often came as ballast in the holds of ships, then were sold off by the cartload at the wharves.</aside>

This walk goes from patch to patch: nine of them, the largest the size of a living room. Wear shoes with thick soles, and walk it soon. The city has been repaving.
