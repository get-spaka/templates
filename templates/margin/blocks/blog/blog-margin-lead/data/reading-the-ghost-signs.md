The best light for reading a ghost sign is the hour after a rain, when the brick darkens and the old paint, which holds less water, stands out pale against it. I learned this from a sign painter who still works, and who reads the old ones the way other people read gravestones.

<aside>He can tell the decade of a sign from its letters alone: the width of an S, whether the serifs were drawn or stenciled.</aside>

This walk takes about an hour and passes eleven signs, from a laundry that closed before the war to a shop that sold, according to its wall, <mark>books, stationery, drawing instruments and slide rules</mark>, all in one place.

## What they are selling

Most ghost signs advertised things you could buy on the next corner: flour, cigars, coal, a hotel with steam heat. They were painted high, to be read from a streetcar, and they lasted because nobody bothered to paint over them once the business had gone.

> A ghost sign is the only advertisement that gets more interesting the longer you ignore it.

Read them while you can. Every year a few more disappear behind new siding, or are painted over by someone who thought they were dirt.
