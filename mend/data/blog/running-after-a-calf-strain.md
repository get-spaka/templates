A calf strain usually happens in the last kilometre, on a hill or a quick change of pace: a sudden pull, like being kicked from behind. Most heal in two to six weeks. The trouble is that many come back, because we start running before the calf is strong enough to take it.

## 1. Walk well first

Walk without a limp for a few days before anything else. If you limp, the calf isn't ready.

## 2. Build strength

Calf raises on both legs, then on one, then on the edge of a step. Aim for 25 slow single-leg raises before you run.

## 3. Hop

Small, quiet hops on the spot, then forward. If hopping hurts, running will.

## 4. Run little and often

Start with walk-run intervals on flat ground every other day, and add a few minutes each time. Hills and speed come last.

If you're stuck at one step for more than a couple of weeks, come and see us: a calf that won't progress usually has a reason.
